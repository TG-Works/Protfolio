01 / Engineering objective

Maximize track learning per dollar.

The build began with a motorcycle that had been stored for roughly five years and already included major chassis modifications. The engineering task is to establish a reliable baseline, understand the mixed-component system, and add only the upgrades that improve safety, feedback, consistency, or usable track data.

  • Reliable operationResolve storage-related fuel, electrical, cooling, and lubrication risks before performance work.
  • Known chassis conditionInspect bearings, suspension, controls, fasteners, and geometry around the GSX-R conversion.
  • Rider fitAdjust seat height, bar position, rearsets, and control angles around the rider's knee and track posture.
  • ServiceabilityKeep the harness, drivetrain, bodywork, and consumables easy to inspect and repair between sessions.
  • Useful dataPrioritize lap timing and session logging over a costly full replacement dashboard.
  • Value disciplineSpend on high-impact reliability and control improvements before premium hardware.

03 / Recommissioning

Diagnose the stored bike before upgrading it.

The initial no-start condition was treated as a system problem. The tank and fuel-pump assembly showed corrosion after long storage, and the diagnostic display initially reported a fault before returning to normal after troubleshooting.

04 / Design decisions

Choose upgrades by effect, not price tag.

DecisionCurrent directionEngineering reason
Display / dataGPS lap timer and logger rather than a full dashCapture useful session data without unnecessary integration cost
QuickshifterEvaluate bidirectional functionality and drivetrain compatibilityImprove shift consistency while protecting the gearbox and maintaining correct load strategy
Drivetrain520 X-ring chain with track-oriented gearingServiceable, readily available, and matched to the build's performance level
Rider positionThicker seat pad and lower clip-on configurationImprove knee angle, leverage, and room to move on the bike
HarnessRemove only confirmed unused circuits and preserve diagnostics / safety logicAvoid weight-saving changes that create unreliable interlocks or hard-to-find electrical faults
BrakesMatch master-cylinder choice to caliper piston area and desired lever feelUse hydraulic ratio and rider feedback instead of selecting by brand alone

05 / Track-readiness roadmap

Earn speed through reliable phases.

PhaseFocusExit condition
01 · Static baselineFuel, charging, fluids, leaks, fan operation, controls, fastenersStarts, charges, reaches temperature safely, and passes a workshop inspection
02 · Rolling validationBrakes, clutch, shifts, chain alignment, suspension movement, steeringShort test operation without leaks, interference, warning codes, or abnormal heat
03 · Rider interfaceSeat, bars, rearsets, levers, control angles, safety wiringComfortable repeatable body position and unambiguous control access
04 · Track baselineTires, pressures, sag, damping notes, brake feel, temperature, session inspectionConsistent sessions with documented setup and no developing faults
05 · Data / iterationLap timer, logger, quickshifter, setup changes, rider feedbackOne change at a time with a clear observation and decision record

Build principle

Reliable laps before expensive parts.

The SV650 is intentionally a practical engineering platform: diagnose first, establish a baseline, document changes, and spend where the rider can actually use the result.
