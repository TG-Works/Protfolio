01 / Engineering problem

Create a repeatable experimental environment.

Battery experiments required controlled mechanical loading and temperature, coordinated instrumentation, traceable data, and safeguards suitable for laboratory operation. The system had to join precision mechanical design with automation and clear operator procedures.

  • Controlled loadingApply and maintain a defined mechanical force or pressure condition.
  • Thermal capabilityIntegrate heating while protecting sensors, interfaces, and surrounding hardware.
  • Instrument coordinationConnect cyclers, power supplies, chambers, sensors, and data acquisition.
  • RepeatabilityUse controlled geometry, calibration, recipes, and documented procedures.
  • SafetyUse interlocks, thresholds, fault handling, and controlled shutdown behavior.
  • TraceabilityLog run conditions and generate data suitable for analysis and reporting.

03 / Mechanical development

Design the experiment into the hardware.

The press and test fixture were not passive structures. Alignment, contact interfaces, gasket behavior, heat flow, force transfer, access, and calibration all affected the quality of the experiment.

04 / Controls & validation

Automate the routine while making faults visible.

Modular instrument control and recipe execution made test conditions more repeatable. QC thresholds and safety checks were designed to interrupt or flag runs when measured behavior departed from the intended envelope.

  • Modular driversSeparate instrument communication from test-sequence logic.
  • Recipe executionDefine a repeatable sequence of setup, control, logging, and completion steps.
  • Live QCCompare measured values with expected limits during operation.
  • Fault handlingProvide explicit responses for loss of communication, limit violations, or operator stops.
  • Analysis pipelineOrganize data for repeatable review, plotting, and reporting.
  • HandoffDocument calibration, operation, maintenance, and known limitations for researchers.

Result

The completed system improved repeatability, traceability, and operator handoff through controlled hardware, instrument automation, structured data, and documented procedures.

Core lesson

Test quality begins with system design.

Mechanical interfaces, sensors, software, safety logic, and operating procedures all shape the validity of an experimental result.
