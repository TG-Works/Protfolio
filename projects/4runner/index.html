01 / Engineering objective

Build capability without compromising the vehicle.

The project is structured as vehicle systems engineering—not a collection of accessories. Every change is evaluated against reliability, mass, cost, serviceability, packaging, strength, manufacturability, and its effect on other systems.

  • Reliable daily operationEstablish a known mechanical baseline before adding loads or complexity.
  • Off-road durabilityImprove clearance and protection while controlling driveline and steering stress.
  • Field serviceabilityUse accessible hardware, documented circuits, and repairable fabrication.
  • Towing readinessManage cooling, braking, stability, payload, and tongue load as one system.
  • Practical packagingPreserve cargo access while integrating tools, recovery equipment, and auxiliary power.
  • Controlled massAvoid stacking heavy armor, storage, batteries, and accessories without a weight budget.

02 / Systems architecture

Seven systems, one vehicle-level plan.

01

Reliability Baseline

Suspension and steering wear items, ignition, seals, timing-belt service, cooling, transmission, differentials, and CV axles.

02

Suspension & Chassis

Approximately 3-inch lift, 33-inch-class tires, wheel clearance, CV angle, steering, alignment, and articulation.

03

Braking

Baseline refresh, pedal feel, lines, pads, rotors, rear drums, and evaluation of a Tundra front-brake conversion.

04

Thermal Management

Cooling-system condition, radiator, transmission cooler, temperature monitoring, and towing heat load.

05

Fabrication

Rock sliders, skid protection, bumpers, tire carrier, roof rack, load paths, weight, clearance, and service access.

06

Auxiliary Electrical

Isolated battery, DC-DC charging, future solar, fuse distribution, lighting, fridge, communications, and charging loads.

07

Cargo & Towing

Lightweight drawers, locking slides, load distribution, recovery gear, track equipment, trailer weight, and stability.

03 / Reliability baseline

Reduce unknowns before increasing capability.

The first phase addressed age- and mileage-related risk. Work is tracked as systems rather than isolated repairs so that suspension, steering, drivetrain, brakes, and cooling can be evaluated together.

Drivetrain / fluidsTransmission, differential, CV axle, seal, and preventive-service workControl leaks, heat, lubrication condition, and field-failure risk

04 / Mini case study

Rock sliders as a load-path and packaging problem.

The slider concept is designed around more than tube appearance. It must distribute impact and vehicle-jacking loads into the frame, protect the rocker, clear the fuel tank and body, preserve ground clearance, and remain practical to fabricate and service.

  • Frame attachmentBolt-on U-plate concept with multiple attachment stations and controlled load distribution.
  • PackagingFuel-tank clearance, rocker coverage, door access, tire clearance, and kick-out placement.
  • StructureSquare structural legs, round outer tube, gusset strategy, plate thickness, and bending resistance.
  • FabricationDOM tube preference, repeatable bends/notches, weld access, simple plates, and replaceable hardware.
DecisionCurrent directionReason to validate
MountingThree frame attachment groups per sideSpread local loads and limit slider rotation
Plate conceptApproximately 3/16-inch steel mounting platesBalance stiffness, weldability, weight, and bolt bearing
Outer geometryFull-length round outer tube with rear kick-outRocker protection, obstacle deflection, and rear-quarter coverage
Open validationLoad cases, deflection, hardware access, and fuel-tank envelopeConvert concept into a build-ready drawing package

Engineering approach

Reliability first. Capability second.

The project demonstrates system-level tradeoffs: every pound, electrical load, suspension change, fabricated component, and towing demand affects the rest of the vehicle.
